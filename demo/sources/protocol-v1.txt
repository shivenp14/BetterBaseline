CLINICAL STUDY PROTOCOL

Study Identifier: TX-2026-01
Study Title: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Investigational Agent TS-101 in Adults with Moderate-to-Severe Chronic Inflammatory Disease
Protocol Version: 1.0 (Baseline)
Date: 2026-01-15
Sponsor: TrialSync Clinical Therapeutics

1. STUDY SYNOPSIS AND OBJECTIVES
1.1 Primary Objective
The primary objective of this study is to evaluate the clinical efficacy of TS-101 compared with placebo in achieving disease remission.

1.2 Secondary Objectives
- Assess safety and tolerability of TS-101.
- Measure patient-reported quality of life and physical functioning.

2. STUDY DESIGN AND POPULATION
This is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group Phase III study. Approximately 240 adult participants will be enrolled across 18 clinical sites and randomized 1:1 to receive either TS-101 (200 mg subcutaneous biweekly) or matching placebo.

3. SCHEDULE OF ASSESSMENTS
Participants will attend scheduled clinic visits for screening, baseline randomization (Week 0), and regular monitoring visits every 4 weeks. Key assessments include physical examinations, laboratory safety panels, pharmacokinetics, and clinical disease activity indices.

section: endpoint
Primary endpoint assessment occurs at week 24.
The primary efficacy estimand is the proportion of participants achieving clinical remission (defined as a reduction in composite inflammatory score >= 50% from baseline) without rescue therapy. All primary statistical inferences will be conducted on the intent-to-treat (ITT) population.

4. SAFETY MONITORING AND REPORTING
Adverse events and serious adverse events will be monitored continuously from informed consent through the end of safety follow-up. An independent Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) will review unblinded safety data at regular intervals.

5. HISTORICAL BENCHMARK
Study assumptions and sample size calculations were informed by historical study CSR-2024-09, which evaluated early-generation biologic comparators under equivalent clinical trial conditions.
