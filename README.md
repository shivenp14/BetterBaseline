# Clinical Trial Dependency Manager

We help clinical-trial teams trace dependencies across fragmented data, documents, and decisions, identify the consequences of changes, and coordinate their resolution. By reducing avoidable delays and inconsistencies, we help important research progress more efficiently.

## Why this matters

Clinical trials involve many documents, datasets, and teams. The same information can appear in several places, and decisions often rely on information maintained elsewhere.

When something changes, the team needs to find everything affected and make sure the right people review it. Doing this manually takes time, and missed updates can leave documents inconsistent or decisions based on outdated information.

Our project gives teams a clear view of these connections and helps them follow changes through to resolution. Reducing this coordination work gives researchers more time to advance clinical trials and develop medicines that can benefit society. Over time, greater efficiency could help teams support more trials.

## How the project works

1. **Bring trial information together.** Teams add the documents and data used to plan, run, and report on a trial.
2. **Connect related information.** The project maps which documents, results, and decisions rely on each other. Users can inspect the source behind each connection and confirm it.
3. **Identify what a change affects.** When information is updated, the project highlights the related work that may need attention and explains why.
4. **Help the right people act.** Each issue goes to the person responsible for reviewing it. They can check the evidence, revise a document, request a new analysis, or confirm that no update is needed.
5. **Track the resolution.** The team can see what remains outstanding, what has been completed, and who reviewed each change. Previous versions remain available so earlier decisions can still be understood.

## An example

A trial changes when an assessment takes place, from week 24 to week 36.

That timing may also appear in the analysis plan, visit schedule, and participant information. The project identifies the connected sections, explains that they depend on the assessment date, and gives their owners specific review tasks.

The team can then make and approve the necessary updates while tracking their progress in one place. A document describing an earlier stage of the trial can retain its original information when appropriate.

The same process applies to data corrections: if a result changes, the project helps identify which analyses and report statements need checking.

## Additional benefit: equitable participation

Trial changes can also affect participants differently. An added visit might require more transport assistance, childcare, or accessible services.

By connecting these needs to the study requirements and support commitments, the project can help coordinators identify new gaps and follow up on the support needed. This extends the core idea to help preserve access as trials evolve.
